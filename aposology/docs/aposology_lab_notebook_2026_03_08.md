## 1. What Is Aposology?

**Aposology** (coined by the author) is the systematic study of absences — treating what is NOT present, NOT recorded, or NOT considered as a positive object of inquiry rather than a deficiency to be corrected.

The core claim:

> In any system where observations are generated by an observer with a theory-laden, socially embedded, historically conditioned perceptual filter, the pattern of absences in the resulting dataset is structured — and that structure contains recoverable information about both the phenomenon being observed AND the observation process itself.

This is distinct from, though related to:
- Taphonomy (structured loss in fossil record — natural processes, does not interrogate the observer)
- Missing data statistics (treats absence as nuisance to correct, not signal to interpret)
- Dark data ecology (notices the problem, lacks the framework)
- Market basket analysis (operates within closed, fully enumerated systems — not open, historically constructed ones)
- Trouillot's archival silences (epistemological depth, but not quantitative)

None of these do what aposology attempts: treating structured absence as a positive object of study, developing formal methods for detecting and characterizing it, and grounding it in systematic analysis of the process generating it.

## 2. The Foundational Epistemological Point

**Presence and absence are not symmetric operations.**

Determining presence requires finding the "summit" — a point, a local maximum, unambiguously itself.

Determining absence requires solving the boundary problem. The mountain does not end — it grades, transitions, becomes foothills, becomes plain. There is no formal stopping point.

*Origin of this insight: ~1984, observing Pooecetes gramineus — a monotypic genus — and asking what a category boundary means when the the category seems complete itself, yet is treated like other categories that include implied context. This question, generalized, became the intellectual seed of aposology. The expansion of the idea resulted from the understanding that in collecting, or observing, the most frequently occurring and common entities are NOT collected or recorded BECAUSE they are common, so paradoxically commoness can be manifested as absence in the record.
If nothing else absence of a commonly occurring entity in a collection record tells something about the NATURE of the collection process and has implications about expectations about the output of that collecting process.*


**The Mt. Rainier formulation:** Standing on the summit, you can say precisely where you are. Walking down, you cannot say precisely when you are no longer on Mt. Rainier. Presence localizes. Absence diffuses.

## 3. Categories of Absence

This taxonomy is central to aposology. Not all absences are equal or equally informative.

### 3a. Absolute Absences
Absences that carry no information because they were never possible given the system's architecture. Example: absence of lichen specimens from the moon. No expectation, no signal.

### 3b. Contingent (Relative) Absences
Absences that are essentially shadows of a presence. These carry information proportional to the strength of the prior expectation that something SHOULD be there.

If a highly common, ecologically well-characterized lichen is absent from a record covering apparently suitable habitat, that absence is nearly screaming. It has **informational density** — a function of how strong the expectation was and how many independent pathways to presence existed and were not taken.

These are the analytically rich absences. They are the heart of aposology.

### 3c. Invisible (Nomenclaturally Unconstituted) Absences
Absences of things that could not have been recorded because the category did not yet exist. The thing was there. The collector walked past it. Not from inattention but because they had no name, no conceptual bucket, no reason to pick it up.

A taxonomic split retroactively creates invisible absences in the historical record. These absences are not recoverable as such — they require nomenclatural reconstruction.

### 3d. Aposological Compounding
Absence can generate expectation of absence that is unrelated to actual absence.
If a collector visits an area that has previously been collected and familiarized themselves with the list of 
taxa previously collect. The goes to the collection area, sees a common taxon and remembers that it was NOT 
included in the list of taxa collected. They can then think,,"I am not going to look stupid by collecting
this common thing, thinking that it is standard practice to not collect a hum drum sample from this area.  Absence in record can perpetuat itself.

A taxon not considered interesting in 1890 is absent from the record. That absence shapes the 1920 collector's expectations — who has no reason to look. Which generates another absence. The shadow deepens not because the taxon isn't there but because the expectation system has progressively less room for it.


The inverse also operates: a taxon collected early, described by a famous lichenologist, becomes "expected" — and therefore found more often — partly because the expectation system is tuned to it.

## 4. The Presence Bias in Science

Scientific work has a profound, pervasive and understandable presence bias operating simultaneously at multiple levels:

**Ontological:** A specimen exists, therefore it is real and recordable. Absence is merely non-occurrence of a presence, not a thing in itself.

**Methodological:** The standard response to absence is "collect more data" — treating absence as measurement artifact rather than signal.

**Incentive:** Careers, publications, and collections are built on presences. Nobody gets a grant to document what isn't there. Nobody names a new absence after themselves.

**Archival:** The herbarium record physically cannot contain an absence. Symbiota / Darwin Core have fields for what was collected, not for what was not. The closest thing could be a note in Remarks saying, for example, something was looked for but not found.. I have never seen sucn a remark.

**Representational:** Absence has no natural cartographic home. Its topography must be invented from scratch.

## 5. Perception as Structured Exclusion

The lichenologist walking through a forest is not a passive recorder. They are an active filter — and the filter was constructed long before they left for the field.

The parallel systems:
- Selective microbiological media does not *find* bacteria — it *constitutes* a subset as findable
- The visual system does not *see* the world — it constitutes a subset as visible  
- The LHC trigger system does not *record* collisions — it constitutes a subset as recordable
- The lichenologist does not *collect* lichens — they constitute a subset as collectable

The filter is not always applied at the moment of encounter. It can be applied upstream, in the design of the system, the training of the observer, the construction of the categories; it can be applied at the moment of encounter, and it can be applied at any time AFTER the encounter.

**The main activity of a lichenologist on a collecting trip is NOT collecting lichens it is NOT collecting lichens. The grammar paradox is INDICATIVE of the state of expectation that the act of collecting IS the act in question, while in fact what mostly happens is NOT COLLECTING.** They see thousands of lichens and do not collect almost all of them. The act of collection is the exception. Structured exclusion is the rule.

## 6. The Waddington Pipeline Framework

### The Two Waddington Modes
1. **The epigenetic landscape** — canalized pathways, attractors, chreods. The landscape of possibilities across which the becoming-a-herbarium-record process rolls. Some valleys are deeply canalized (well-funded expedition to a well-known flora). Others are never explored.
2. **Operational Research mode** (anti-submarine warfare) — systematic decomposition of a complex problem into operative components, asking: what are the actual steps, what is measurable at each step, and where does the system leak? This is the primary mode for pipeline analysis.

The Waddington ASW insight: the pattern of where submarines were NOT found contained information about where search effort was applied vs. where submarines actually were. Absence of detections was not just failure — it was a dataset and a useful dataset. Structurally identical to the lichen problem.

### The Pipeline Is Not a Pipeline — It Is a Series of Filters

Each filter has its own logic of inclusion and exclusion, operating at different timescales, with different degrees of visibility, and generating its own aposological residue.

The system has two registers at each step:
- **Positive face:** what happens when the step proceeds
- **Aposological shadow:** what is excluded, deferred, lost, or never initiated

### Fundamental Steps (Draft — Subject to Revision)

| Step | Description | Primary Vectors |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| S1 | Formation of a lichenological subject | Training, credentialing, institutional access, funding, social networks |
| S2 | Conceptualization of collecting space | Empire, infrastructure, language, prior literature, aesthetic of the interesting |
| S3 | Authorization of a collecting event | Funding sources, institutional priorities, permit regimes, personal networks |
| S4 | Execution of travel and site access | Terrain, road networks, land tenure, access regimes, seasonality, time pressure |
| S5 | The collecting act itself | Observer search image, substrate preferences, taxon expectations, size bias, fertility bias |
| S6 | Specimen fate and institutional intake | Shipping, storage, loss, institutional acceptance, curation quality |
| S7 | Identification and nomenclatural assignment | Taxonomic framework current at time, expertise, correspondence networks |
| S8 | Physical curation and preservation | Funding, institutional stability, disaster, institutional priority |
| S9 | Digitization and data entry | Digitization project scope, volunteer quality, OCR error, field mapping decisions |
| S10 | Aggregation and portal ingestion | Darwin Core mapping, deduplication decisions, taxonomy normalization |
| S11 | Data use and analytical visibility | Which questions are asked of the data, which analyses are considered legitimate |

**Critical note:** Steps S1-S3 occur entirely before departure. They receive almost no analytical attention in the literature, yet they establish the expectation infrastructure that may govern and certainly can influence all downstream steps. Pre-departure may be where the deepest and least-examined biases originate.
These biases may be unconcious, unexpressed, partly expressed, and can be subject to frequent revision.
It will be rare to find instances where these a priori orientations are expressed, or recorded, and it is not 
reasonable to expect that to change.

### The Expectation System

Expectations are the master filter. They are:
- **Recursive:** formed from prior data, which was itself the product of prior expectations
- **Self-referential:** each generation of data partially constructs the expectations generating the next generation
- **Socially negotiated:** not just individually constructed but institutionally stabilized
- **Propagating:** dominant collectors' expectations spread through students, publications, and the reputation of places

By the time a lichenologist stands in front of a substrate, the question of what they *see* may have been 
largely been determined by everything that happened prior.

## 7. The Chromatographic / Waddington Surface Synthesis

The world as a combination Waddington surface and chromatogram:

A bewildering array of attractors, ridges, valleys, affinities and repellants. Complex moieties consisting of arrays of temporal associations and bonds move through high-dimensional n-space with trajectories and events that flow from that hyperdimensional space with non-zero epsilon.

The lichenologist, the specimen, the herbarium record, the absence — these are not just balls rolling down a landscape. They are **moieties with affinities**. They bind to some things and not others. They move at different rates through the same medium. They separate or co-migrate depending on their properties and the properties of what surrounds them.

**Non-zero epsilon** is crucial: nothing is purely determined. There is always a small but real probability of the unexpected trajectory. The collector who goes to the Permian Basin. The absence that turns out to be presence. This is why aposology is possible — the system is lawful but not closed.

## 8. Empirical Results to Date

### 8a. The Absence Matrix (84 × 48)
- 84 high-density counties (≥3000 species-level records) as calibration set — the closest available approximation to "what a well-sampled lichen flora looks like"
- Top-10 taxa per county identified
- All 48 CONUS states scored by degree of absence of those taxa
- Hall's distance matrix computed on absence profiles
- Ward's hierarchical clustering, k=5

**Result:** Geographically coherent state zones recovered from pure absence algebra, with zero geographic information in the algorithm. The algorithm knows nothing of geography. The geography emerges from the structure of the absences.

**Key diagnostic anomalies (interpretable, not random):**
- South Dakota: Wetmore collector effect
- Delaware: severe systematic undercollection
- Georgia: Appalachian bias in high-density counties

**Robustness:** Increasing counties from 36→84 did not destroy structure. Low-frequency collector bias (avoidance of common taxa) is spatially autocorrelated, not white noise — it preserves large-scale structure.

### 8b. The Grand Void — Topography of Absence
10×10 km grid across CONUS48. Each cell colored by distance to nearest cell containing any lichen record.

**The Epitome of Ignorance:** Near Midland, Texas (Permian Basin). Not a peak — the floor of a deep basin. The point maximally distant from any lichenological knowledge. Likely explained by: private land, oil extraction infrastructure, no institutional collecting presence, access regime exclusion.

**The Centroid of Absence:** Northern Kansas border region. The center of gravity of the entire void system — geometrically distinct from the epitome.

**The Grand Void is not an ecological phenomenon. It is a sociological and epistemological phenomenon.** It is the spatial footprint of a century of collecting decisions, funding structures, institutional locations, road networks, and inherited expectations about where interesting lichens live.

### 8c. Migration of the Centroid of Knowledge
Decadal MEDIAN centroid of all lichen collecting activity, 1826-2025.

- Begins in northeastern US (institutional origin of American lichenology)
- Long westward migration tracking continental opening, railroad access, institutional expansion
- Anomalous northeastern lurch ca. 1890-1910 (likely specific collector/institutional event)
- Major 20th century westward surge — Pacific coast and Arizona collections
- 2010-2020: centroid far south and west; record volumes largest

**The centroid of knowledge and the centroid of ignorance are nearly coincident over Kansas.**

This is not paradoxical. Knowledge and ignorance are not opposites occupying different spaces — they are complements generated by the same process. They are poles in the space of epistemic reach. When projected onto a flat map, they appear nearly coincident because both are children of the same bounded, historically contingent sphere of lichenological attention.

### 8d. Top 4 Collectors — The Social Structure of a Discipline as Geography
Four collectors (Wetmore, Lendemer, Nash, Harris) account for a remarkable share of CONUS48 records. Their collecting centroids partition the continent — not by design but by the social structure of institutional affiliation, funding, and career geography.

The white diamond (centroid of ALL records) floats over Kansas — pulled in four directions simultaneously by four gravitational masses, sitting over some of the most undersampled terrain on the continent.

**Keweenaw County** (~19,000 records, tiny area) vs. **Eastern Oregon & Washington** (~21,000 records, enormous area): equivalent record counts, ~1/1000th the land area. Keweenaw is special not because of what it is but because of who decided it was special — Thompson and Wetmore — and who followed, and who trained students there.

## 9. The Nomenclatural Robustness Point

Lichenologists often object that CLH data is too nomenclaturally noisy for meaningful pattern analysis. This objection assumes the signal lives in precise taxon identity.

**Absence patterns are nomenclaturally buffered.** A synonymy or split changes what a presence record is called — it does not change the absence of whatever-we-are-calling-it from Kansas. The ecological and collector-historical reality encoded in an absence is indifferent to nomenclatural revision.

The absence clustering works without nomenclatural cleanup. This is not a limitation — it is evidence that the signal operates at a scale where nomenclatural noise is low-frequency relative to ecological and collector-historical structure.

Rich Hickey principle applies: data accumulates, it doesn't change. All taxonomy is "wrong" or will be soon enough. The data is the data.

## 10. The Herbarium Architecture Problem

Herbaria are presence accumulation systems. Every design decision — the physical cabinet, the accession record, the database schema, Symbiota, Darwin Core — is built around the assumption that the unit of information is a specimen that exists.

**There is no institutional home for a qualified absence record.**

A carefully executed survey — observer identity and expertise, date and duration of search, geographic coordinates and area searched, target taxon, substrate and habitat examined, confidence level for the absence determination — this is a richer and more epistemically serious record than most presence records currently contain.

It cannot be deposited in a herbarium. It has no accession number. It cannot be cited. It evaporates.

This is a structural incompatibility between the most important practical implication of aposology and the entire institutional architecture of the discipline.

**Comparison:** eBird's complete checklists (observer records everything seen AND implicitly everything not seen on a defined route in a defined time window) was a methodological revolution precisely because it created a framework for qualified absence. Lichenology has no equivalent.

## 12. Philosophical Foundation (Working Formulation)

Aposology has three registers:

**Empirical:** Structured absence has recoverable geometry. Absence contains information. The zone maps are the proof of concept.

**Analytical:** The Waddington pipeline schematic — systematic decomposition of the herbarium data generation process into steps, vectors, and aposological shadows, including the pre-field expectation infrastructure.

**Epistemological:** Perception is structured exclusion. Data systems are self-referential. Absence compounds. The herbarium is not a sample of the world — it is a sample of a historically and socially constructed version of the world. All presence data is the visible tip of a structured iceberg of absences. The tip cannot be properly interpreted without a theory of the iceberg.

**The shadow metaphor:** Absence is the shadow of presence. If presence is spatially structured — and we know it is — then its complement cannot be random. Shadows preserve shape, blur edges, distort proportions, but still encode the object. The absence zones are silhouettes of real regions. Not perfect mirrors. Recognizable silhouettes.

**Formally:** If P(x) has spatial autocorrelation, then its complement 1-P(x) must also have spatial autocorrelation — unless detection noise overwhelms signal. The experiments suggest it doesn't.
